### Topics of Interest

* Open-weight foundation models and small language models for agentic AI
* Efficient reasoning, planning, and test-time scaling for agents
* Memory, retrieval, tool use, and multi-agent collaboration
* Resource-constrained development and deployment, privacy-preserving AI, and edge agents
* Benchmarking and evaluation of open and efficient agentic systems
* Applications of open and efficient agents in various domains such as science, industry, and society

### Target Audience

The workshop targets researchers, master and PhD students, practitioners, and industry professionals interested in developing capable agentic AI systems under realistic computational, economic, and deployment constraints. It will be particularly relevant to researchers working in machine learning, artificial intelligence, natural language processing, reinforcement learning, multi-agent systems, foundation models, trustworthy AI, and AI systems.

The workshop is expected to attract participants from academia, industry, startups, and public-sector organizations who are interested in open-weight foundation models, efficient reasoning, memory- and retrieval-augmented agents, and resource-efficient deployment. The workshop is especially relevant to those in the Asia-Pacific region, where access to frontier-scale computational infrastructure and proprietary models may be limited, creating a strong need for accessible, affordable, and deployable agentic AI solutions.

### Submission and Review Plan

We plan to solicit full papers (up to 8 pages excluding references) as well as short papers (up to 4 pages) reporting preliminary results, emerging ideas, and position papers. The workshop welcomes both mature research contributions and promising early-stage work aligned with the workshop theme.

At least two reviewers will review each submission based on relevance, originality, technical quality, clarity, reproducibility, and potential impact. Particular attention will be given to research that advances open and efficient agentic AI, especially under realistic computational, deployment, or resource constraints. Paper submissions will be reviewed by a geographically diverse, multi-institutional Program Committee to ensure fairness and minimize conflicts of interest. The Organizing Committee will make review assignments after screening author lists for potential conflicts.

Accepted papers will be presented as oral presentations or posters, providing opportunities for in-depth discussion and feedback. The workshop aims to foster interaction between researchers from academia, industry, and government, while encouraging participation from emerging research communities across the Asia-Pacific region.

### Program Committee

The Program Committee will be coordinated by Hung Le (Deakin University), Haripriya Harikumar (University of Manchester), and Long Dang (Posts and Telecommunications Institute of Technology), leveraging their experience as Area Chairs, Senior Program Committee members, Meta-Reviewers, and Reviewers at conferences including NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, ACL, and AAMAS. Additional Program Committee members will be recruited from academia, industry, and government research organizations with expertise in foundation models, agentic AI, planning and reasoning, AI systems, and trustworthy AI. Each submission will receive at least two reviews.
